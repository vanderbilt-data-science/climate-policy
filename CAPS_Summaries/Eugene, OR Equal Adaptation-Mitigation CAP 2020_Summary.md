**Potential Unforeseen Maladaptive Climate Policies**

1. **Policy: Tree Ordinance and Urban Forestry**

   - **Climate Threat Addressed:**
     The policy aims to address increased temperatures and reduced ecological function due to climate change by increasing urban tree canopy and preserving existing mature trees.

   - **Resilience, Mitigation, or Adaptation Option:**
     Resilience option includes actions such as updating the City’s Urban Forestry Plan, instituting a Tree Ordinance to enforce the preservation of existing mature trees, reviewing planning permits for tree removal, and creating a comprehensive canopy development and maintenance strategy. *(CAP 2.0 Page 73)*

   - **Potential for Maladaptation:**
     Although increasing urban tree canopy can help mitigate urban heat islands and improve air quality, it may inadvertently lead to maladaptation if not carefully managed. For instance, enforcing strict tree preservation without considering appropriate tree species selection could result in increased vulnerability to pests or diseases that thrive under warmer conditions. Moreover, if neighborhoods have control over tree removal but lack the expertise to make ecologically sound decisions, it may lead to ineffective outcomes or resistance from property owners due to perceived restrictions on land use.

2. **Policy: Prohibition or Limitation of Natural Gas in New City Buildings**

   - **Climate Threat Addressed:**
     The policy targets the reduction of emissions from natural gas, which contributes to GHGs exacerbating climate change.

   - **Resilience, Mitigation, or Adaptation Option:**
     Mitigation option includes prohibiting or severely limiting natural gas in all new City buildings and major renovations of City-owned buildings. *(CAP 2.0 Page 63)*

   - **Potential for Maladaptation:**
     While the move aims at reducing emissions from fossil fuels, the abrupt shift from natural gas might lead to maladaptation if alternative energy sources are not sufficiently reliable or if the transition costs disproportionately burden low-income households. If the electric grid is not prepared to handle increased demand, it could result in energy insecurity, particularly during extreme weather events when heating or cooling needs are critical.

3. **Policy: Phase-Out of Single-Use Plastics**

   - **Climate Threat Addressed:**
     The policy targets reducing waste and its associated emissions which contribute to climate change.

   - **Resilience, Mitigation, or Adaptation Option:**
     Mitigation option involves phasing out most or all single-use plastics over ten years. *(CAP 2.0 Page 63)*

   - **Potential for Maladaptation:**
     The prohibition of single-use plastics without adequate and accessible alternatives may result in maladaptation due to increased use of alternative materials. If the alternatives have greater environmental impacts across their lifecycle or if composting and recycling infrastructure cannot cope with these alternatives, it could lead to increased environmental degradation and costs, potentially negating the intended reduction in waste emissions. Additionally, if businesses and consumers do not transition to sustainable alternatives, the demand for environmental resources could increase, exacerbating climate impacts.

Each of these policies addresses significant threats and intends to build resilience or reduce emissions. However, without careful implementation and context consideration, there is a risk of unintended consequences that could hinder effectiveness or exacerbate vulnerabilities.
